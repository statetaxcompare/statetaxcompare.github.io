The verdict

On income tax: at lower and middle incomes, Ontario's progressive rates actually start lower than Alberta's flat 10% — so modest earners sometimes pay less provincial income tax in Ontario. At higher incomes, Alberta pulls ahead thanks to its lower top combined rate (~48% vs ~53.5%).

On everyday spending: Alberta's lack of a provincial sales tax is the real equalizer — Ontario's 13% HST applies to nearly everything you buy, while Alberta charges only the 5% federal GST.

On housing: this is where the gap widens most. Toronto-area rent and home prices run well above Calgary and Edmonton, which is the single biggest factor behind Alberta's lower overall cost of living.
